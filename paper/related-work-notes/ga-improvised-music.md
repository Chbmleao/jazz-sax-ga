## Methodology 
- AMUSE = A MUSical Evolutionary assistant
- tournament selection with a tour size of four
- population size is one third of the chromosome length

### Representation
- 4 indicates the fourth note in the corresponding scale
- 0 indicates a rest
- the maximum allowed allele value indicates a hold event that makes the previous note to continue

### Objectives and the fitness function
- f1 - chord note
- f2 - relationships between notes
- f3 - directions of notes
- f4 - beginning note
- f5 - ending note
- f6 - over fifth
- f7 - drastic duration change
- f8 - rest proportion
- f9 - hold event proportion
- f10 - pattern matching

## Results
- first part is a Turing test
  - two MIDI formatted music files are given to the participants
  - both files share the same melodic substructure, but they carry different solos
  - one of the solos is generated by an amateur musician
  - the other solo is generated by the GA
  - the particpants are asked to find the correct author of each solo
  - the participants could not diferentiate the human work from the computers work, because 52% of them provided wrong answers
- second part
  - observe whether the GA improves the initial population of melodies or not
  - five different MIDI formatted music files are generated by using Band-in-a-box software 
  - use one solo from the initial population and another one obtained after the 1000 generation with a higher fitness
  - the participants ranked all the solos as they like
- third part
  - the relative importance of each objective composing the fitness function is investigated
  - six different music files are generated by amuse, using the same musical substructure
  - each one exclude one of the objectives
